Sound Waves

A vibrating object makes sound — but how does that sound actually TRAVEL through the air to reach your ear? It travels as a wave: tiny compressions and rarefactions sweeping through the air, even though the air molecules themselves barely move.

You now know how sound travels — as a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions through a material medium. The particles don't move with the wave; they oscillate in place. Sound needs a medium, light doesn't, and that's why we can see distant stars but never hear them.
